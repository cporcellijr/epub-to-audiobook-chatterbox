# title: Letters from the Lighthouse
# characters: Nell Gray=female, Captain Ferris=male, Dr. Amos Vale=male, Aunt Ruth=female
# aliases: Nell Gray=Nell; Captain Ferris=Ferris, the captain; Dr. Amos Vale=Dr. Vale, the doctor, Amos; Aunt Ruth=Ruth

The supply boat came on the first Thursday of the month, weather permitting, and in March the weather rarely permitted. So when Nell Gray saw the sail from the gallery she ran down all ninety-two steps without touching the rail.

«Aunt Ruth»"Slow down," called her aunt from the kitchen. «Aunt Ruth»"Whoever it is will still be there when you get to the bottom."

«Nell Gray»"It's the boat!"

«Aunt Ruth»"It's a boat. There's more than one in the world."

But it was the boat. Captain Ferris brought it in under the lee of the rock with the ease of a man tying his shoe, and by the time Nell reached the jetty he had a line over the post and a sack over his shoulder.

«Captain Ferris»"Morning, Miss Gray. You've grown."

«Nell Gray»"You say that every month."

«Captain Ferris»"And every month it's true. Where's your aunt?"

«Nell Gray»"Making you complain about her coffee."

«Captain Ferris»"I don't complain. I remark." He handed her the sack. «Captain Ferris»"Careful with that. There's a passenger's things in it."

Nell stopped. Passengers did not come to the light. Nobody came to the light but Ferris, and the inspector once a year, and the gulls.

«Nell Gray»"What passenger?"

«Captain Ferris»"Him."

A man was climbing out of the boat behind him, awkwardly, with a doctor's bag in one hand. He was younger than Nell had expected a doctor to be, and greener than anyone she had ever seen.

«Dr. Amos Vale»"Good morning," he said, and sat down on the jetty. «Dr. Amos Vale»"Forgive me. I'll stand in a moment."

«Nell Gray»"Are you ill?"

«Dr. Amos Vale»"I'm a doctor. We're never ill. We're merely at sea."

«Captain Ferris»"He was at sea for eleven miles, Miss Gray, and he was sick for ten of them. I've never seen a man so committed."

«Dr. Amos Vale»"Thank you, Captain."

«Captain Ferris»"Don't mention it."

Aunt Ruth had come down by then, wiping her hands on her apron, and she looked at the doctor on the jetty and then at Ferris.

«Aunt Ruth»"What have you brought me?"

«Captain Ferris»"Dr. Vale. From the town. He's got a letter for you from the Board."

«Aunt Ruth»"The Board writes to me twice a year, and both times it's to tell me the oil is late."

«Dr. Amos Vale»"It isn't about the oil, Mrs. Gray."

«Aunt Ruth»"Miss Gray. My brother was the Mr. Gray. I'm the one who stayed." She held out her hand. «Aunt Ruth»"Letter."

He gave it to her. She read it standing on the jetty in the wind, and Nell watched her face and could not read it at all.

«Nell Gray»"What does it say?"

«Aunt Ruth»"It says the Board has appointed a medical officer to the outer lights," said Ruth slowly, «Aunt Ruth»"and that he is to inspect the keeper and her household, and report on their fitness to continue."

«Nell Gray»"Their fitness?"

«Dr. Amos Vale»"It's a formality," said the doctor, getting to his feet at last. «Dr. Amos Vale»"They're doing it everywhere. Eyes, chest, that sort of thing."

«Aunt Ruth»"And if my eyes are old and my chest is what it is?"

He did not answer that. Ferris coughed and became very interested in his rope.

«Nell Gray»"They'd put us off the light," said Nell. «Nell Gray»"That's what it means. Isn't it?"

«Dr. Amos Vale»"I don't decide that."

«Nell Gray»"But you write the report."

«Dr. Amos Vale»"Yes."

The wind came round the rock and pulled at all four of them. Ruth folded the letter along its creases and put it in her pocket, the way she put away everything she did not intend to think about until dark.

«Aunt Ruth»"Well," she said. «Aunt Ruth»"You'd better come up and inspect us. Ferris, you'll want your coffee."

«Captain Ferris»"I'll want to remark on it, certainly."

They climbed the steps. The doctor counted them under his breath and lost count at sixty. In the kitchen Ruth set out four cups, and the doctor opened his bag, and Nell sat on the window seat with her knees drawn up and watched him.

«Dr. Amos Vale»"Miss Gray. The elder Miss Gray. If you'd sit here, in the light."

«Aunt Ruth»"I've sat in the light for thirty years, doctor. I can manage another five minutes."

He looked in her eyes with a little glass, and listened to her chest, and asked her to read the words on a card, which she did, all of them, including the small ones at the bottom that he said were not part of the test.

«Dr. Amos Vale»"Your sight is remarkable."

«Aunt Ruth»"It's a lighthouse. Sight is the job."

«Dr. Amos Vale»"And the chest?"

«Aunt Ruth»"Is what it is. It'll see me out."

«Dr. Amos Vale»"That's rather what the Board is asking about."

Ruth looked at him for a long moment, and then she laughed, which Nell had not heard her do since the autumn.

«Aunt Ruth»"You're an honest one. They don't usually send an honest one."

«Dr. Amos Vale»"I don't think they knew."

«Captain Ferris»"They'll find out," said Ferris from the doorway. «Captain Ferris»"Honesty gets about."

«Nell Gray»"What about me?" said Nell. «Nell Gray»"Am I to be inspected?"

«Dr. Amos Vale»"You're not the keeper."

«Nell Gray»"I trim the wicks. I wind the clockwork. I've stood the night watch since I was twelve."

«Aunt Ruth»"Nell."

«Nell Gray»"It's true."

«Dr. Amos Vale»"It's also against the regulations, which I am not going to write down." He closed his bag. «Dr. Amos Vale»"Here is what I am going to write. That the keeper's sight is exceptional. That her chest is weak and should be seen again in six months. That the light is kept better than any I have inspected on this coast, and that the household is fit."

«Aunt Ruth»"You've inspected one."

«Dr. Amos Vale»"Then it's true of all of them."

Ruth poured the coffee. Ferris tasted it and remarked on it. Outside the window the sea had turned the colour of slate, and the first drops of rain struck the glass.

«Captain Ferris»"That's the weather," he said. «Captain Ferris»"If I don't go now I don't go till Saturday."

«Dr. Amos Vale»"Go."

«Captain Ferris»"You're coming with me."

«Dr. Amos Vale»"I have to see the chest again in six months. I may as well start now." He sat down at the table. «Dr. Amos Vale»"If Miss Gray will have me until Saturday."

«Aunt Ruth»"Which Miss Gray?"

«Dr. Amos Vale»"Either. Both."

Ruth looked at Nell, and Nell looked at the rain, and neither of them said no.

«Captain Ferris»"Saturday, then," said Ferris, and went down the ninety-two steps whistling, and the boat was a white scrap on the grey water before anyone thought to wave.
