The market opened before dawn on the shortest day, and Hana had been awake for an hour before that, because Grandfather did not believe in lying in bed while there was ice to be broken.

«Old Ivo»"Boots," he said from the doorway.

«Hana»"I have boots on."

«Old Ivo»"Other boots. Those let the water in."

«Hana»"Those are the only boots I have, Grandfather."

«Old Ivo»"Then we'll buy some. After."

They walked down through the sleeping streets with the cart between them. Old Ivo pulled and Hana pushed, and the wheels made the only sound in the world.

«Hana»"After what?"

«Old Ivo»"After we've sold enough to pay for them. That's how buying works."

«Hana»"That's not how you bought the cart."

«Old Ivo»"The cart was a special case."

The square was already half full of stalls. Marta the baker had the corner by the well, as she always did, and her ovens had been going since midnight; the smell reached them before the light did.

«Marta»"Ivo! You're late."

«Old Ivo»"It's not yet six."

«Marta»"And I've sold forty loaves. Hana, come here, you're blue. Eat this."

«Hana»"I can't pay for it."

«Marta»"Did I ask you to? Eat." She pushed a roll into Hana's hands, still hot. «Marta»"Your grandfather can pay me in candles."

«Old Ivo»"My candles are worth two of your rolls."

«Marta»"Then you'll owe me one."

They set up beside her. Ivo's candles were tallow and beeswax, plain and coloured, tall ones for churches and stubs for kitchens, and Hana laid them out in rows by size while her grandfather argued with Marta about the price of flour, which he did every year and never won.

The stranger came just after sunrise.

He wore a hood pulled low and a coat too thin for the season, and he stood at the end of the stall for a long time without speaking, looking at the candles as though they were something else.

«unknown»"How much for the tall ones?"

«Hana»"A penny each. Three for two."

«unknown»"And the red?"

«Hana»"Those are two pennies. The dye is dear."

He took a red one and held it up to the light and turned it, slowly, the way Master Ivo did when he checked a wick.

«unknown»"Who made these?"

«Hana»"My grandfather."

«unknown»"Ask him where he learned."

Hana looked round. Ivo had his back to them, deep in the flour argument, and did not hear.

«Hana»"He learned from his father. Everyone knows that."

«unknown»"Everyone knows a great deal." He put the candle down and laid two pennies beside it, and then, after a moment, a third. «unknown»"That's for the roll you didn't pay for."

«Hana»"How did you..."

But he was already walking away, and the crowd closed behind him, and Hana stood with three pennies in her hand and a cold feeling that had nothing to do with her boots.

«Marta»"Who was that?"

«Hana»"I don't know. He asked where Grandfather learned to make candles."

«Marta»"Did he now." Marta wiped her hands and did not look at Hana. «Marta»"Ivo. Ivo, turn round."

«Old Ivo»"I'm busy losing an argument."

«Marta»"There was a man asking after you."

Ivo turned round then. He looked where Hana pointed, at the place in the crowd where the hood had been.

«Old Ivo»"What did he look like?"

«Hana»"I couldn't see. He had a hood. His voice was..." She stopped. «Hana»"He knew about the roll."

«Old Ivo»"What roll?"

«Marta»"The roll I gave her. He couldn't have seen it. He wasn't here."

Old Ivo was quiet for a long time. The market went on around them, loud and bright and ordinary.

«Old Ivo»"Pack up," he said.

«Hana»"We've only sold six candles."

«Old Ivo»"Pack up, Hana."

«Marta»"Ivo, it's the shortest day. It's your best day."

«Old Ivo»"I know what day it is." He was already lifting the trays back into the cart, not gently. «Old Ivo»"Marta, I'll settle with you next week."

«Marta»"You'll settle with me now, by telling me what's wrong."

He stopped with a tray in his hands. Hana had never seen her grandfather afraid, and she did not know at first that this was what she was seeing.

«Old Ivo»"My father didn't make candles," he said. «Old Ivo»"He kept a lighthouse. I never told anyone that. Not here. Not in forty years."

«Hana»"Then how..."

«Old Ivo»"That's what I'd like to know."

They pushed the cart home through streets that were full now, and every hooded coat made Hana's heart jump. At the door Ivo stopped and looked back down the hill.

«Hana»"Grandfather. Are we in trouble?"

«Old Ivo»"I don't know yet."

«Hana»"He paid for the candle. And the roll. That doesn't sound like trouble."

«Old Ivo»"No," said Ivo. «Old Ivo»"It sounds like someone who wants me to know he could have taken them and didn't."

He went in. Hana stood a moment longer, and then she looked down at the three pennies in her palm, and saw for the first time that one of them was not a penny at all, but a small brass disc stamped with a tower and a single word she could not read.

«Hana»"Grandfather," she said. «Hana»"You'd better come and look at this."

«Old Ivo»"Boots first."

«Hana»"Grandfather."

And then, because her voice had changed, he came.
